# Church software converges, leaving FOSS gaps

*Research report written on 15 November 2026 to scope LibreEkklesia. The compact ID checklist derived from it is `feature-catalog.md`.*

Church management software has converged on one shared core, so LibreEkklesia gains little by reinventing it and a great deal by choosing its edges well. Across the more than 70 commercial and open source products reviewed, almost every suite ships household-aware people records, groups, attendance, security-coded child check-in, paid event registration, volunteer scheduling, fund-designated giving with statements, bulk email and SMS, and a member portal or app; the real differences sit in fund accounting and payroll, website and media, automation depth, regional tax handling, tradition-specific records and, since 2025, AI. The open source side has no equivalent: online giving, secure check-in, volunteer scheduling, worship planning, workflows, communications, a member app and multi-campus are nowhere found together under an OSI licence in a package that is easy to self-host. Rock RMS comes closest but is source-available and Windows-only, ChurchApps B1 is SaaS-first with self-hosting in beta, Churchit is a year old and tied to the Frappe platform, and ChurchCRM still lacks payments, SMS, workflows and multi-campus. Compliance works as a feature generator, not an add-on: tax law dictates receipt and declaration records, card and bank-debit rules dictate hosted payment fields and stored mandates, data-protection law dictates consent, directory privacy and erasure tooling, and child-safety practice dictates matched pickup codes, ratios and expiring credentials. Buyers rank ease of use, price, reputation and data security above feature depth, and their loudest complaints are fragmented data, steep learning curves, rigid reporting, opaque pricing and lock-in. The 2025–2026 release wave centred on AI as an interface to existing records, kanban follow-up workflows, in-app chat with child-safety rules, tap giving and enforced two-step verification. The catalog below tags **396 features across 24 functional areas** as standard, differentiating or rare, so the project can pick a core that reaches parity and a short list of edges where open source can lead.

## One shared core defines the category; the edges decide the purchase

Buyer guides and vendor pages describe the same product. Category membership on G2 is easy to earn: any product with features built for religious organisations, some handling of money, members or events, and a way to message the congregation qualifies, and **374 products** currently do ([G2](https://www.g2.com/categories/church-management)). Capterra, Software Advice and The Lead Pastor each publish their own checklist, yet the lists reduce to eight areas that recur in all of them: people and households, giving, attendance and child check-in, groups, volunteer scheduling, events with calendar and facilities, communications, and reporting ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/church-management-software); [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/church/); [The Lead Pastor](https://www.theleadpastor.com/tools/best-church-management-software)). Accounting is the one area the guides disagree on, and Capterra's filters now let buyers screen for an "AI Copilot", a sign that AI has entered the comparison grid without yet becoming a must-have ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/church-management-software)).

Product pages confirm the convergence and show where it stops. Among the eight market-leading suites, fund accounting and payroll exist only in Realm and the Ministry Brands line ([Realm](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/tools/accounting/); [Amplify](https://www.ministrybrands.com/accounting)). Native worship planning exists in Planning Center, Pushpay, Subsplash and Amplify but not in Realm, MinistryPlatform or TouchPoint ([Planning Center](https://www.planningcenter.com/services); [Realm](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/tools/integrations/); [MinistryPlatform](https://www.acstechnologies.com/ministryplatform/migration/); [TouchPoint](https://www.touchpointsoftware.com/features)). Website, media hosting and streaming are bundled most fully by Subsplash and Amplify ([Subsplash](https://www.subsplash.com/product/one); [Amplify](https://www.ministrybrands.com/bundle-complete)). The low-cost tier repeats the pattern at a lower price: ChurchTrac puts fund accounting, a website builder, an app and check-in into every plan from **$29 a month**, while 50 people is the ceiling of ChMeetings' free tier, which includes people, groups, events, giving records and an app but not bulk messaging, child check-in or paid events ([ChurchTrac](https://www.churchtrac.com/pricing); [ChMeetings](https://www.chmeetings.com/?p=32233)).

Several names changed status during the research window, which matters when feature lists are compared. The Breeze name disappeared on 11 November 2025, when Tithely folded the product into Tithely Church Management ([Tithely](https://get.tithe.ly/blog/breezechms-is-integrated-into-tithely-church-management-heres-what-you-need-to-know)). Amplify is now the only church platform Ministry Brands promotes: FellowshipOne and ShelbyNext are missing from its machine-readable site index, and their own feature pages date from 2020–2021; no end-of-life notice was found, so their entries below describe a legacy feature set ([Ministry Brands](https://www.ministrybrands.com/llms.txt); [FellowshipOne](https://www.fellowshipone.com/church-management/check-in-systems/)). Pushpay began rebuilding ChurchStaq and ParishStaq in June 2026 ([Pushpay](https://hub.pushpay.com/staq-transformation/)). Ownership shifted as well: the Vanco and ACS Technologies merger was announced in December 2025, after Roper Technologies' **$800 million** agreement in July to buy Subsplash ([Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/vanco-acs-technologies-merge-to-court-churches/807481/); [Roper Technologies](https://mfn.se/all/a/roper-technologies/roper-technologies-announces-second-quarter-financial-results-and-acquisition-of-subsplash-increasing-full-year-guidance-593ba8b0)). Website products thinned out too: Faithlife withdrew its Sites product on 30 June 2023, Ministry Brands is steering Clover Sites customers to Amplify, and a security issue took the Sermon Manager plugin off WordPress.org on 3 December 2025 ([Logos](https://www.logos.com/grow/faithlife-strategic-direction-moving-forward/); [Ministry Brands](https://ministrybrands.com/clover); [WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/sermon-manager-for-wordpress/)).

The catalog uses three prevalence labels. **Standard** marks a feature found in most products of both the market-leader and the small-church tier, the level a new product needs for parity. **Differentiating** marks a feature offered by a sizeable minority or by several leaders, usually sold as a reason to choose the product or reserved for a higher plan. **Rare** marks a feature seen in only one to a few products. The labels are judgments drawn from the product pages, changelogs and repositories the research covered, not measured market share. Vendor pages are marketing copy, so a product missing from a row means the feature was not described on the pages read, not that the product lacks it. Each product name links to the page that documents the feature. Three limits apply throughout: nothing was tested hands-on, no Asia-focused product was verified, and no survey measures how many churches use any individual feature.

## The feature catalog: 24 functional areas, each feature tagged by prevalence

Of the 396 features catalogued, **105 are standard, 108 differentiating and 183 rare**. Read together, the standard rows describe the parity floor that every credible product meets: household-aware profiles with custom fields; rule-based saved lists; multi-step follow-up pipelines fed by forms; a group finder with leader-taken attendance; kiosk and app check-in with printed security labels and allergy alerts; paid registration with custom questions; volunteer scheduling with block-out dates and accept or decline; recurring, text and wallet giving with pledges, batch entry and statements; email and SMS; and a member portal with a privacy-aware directory. The differentiating rows are where vendors compete, and the rare rows are a menu of ideas, many of them cheap to build, that only one or two products have tried.

### People and households: custom fields and self-refreshing lists are universal

Every product stores people in households and lets a church add its own fields; the differences lie in how lists are built and how clean the data stays. Planning Center gives its people database away with unlimited records, which sets the expectation that the core record costs nothing ([Planning Center](https://www.planningcenter.com/pricing)). Rule-based lists that refresh themselves are universal, and several vendors added plain-language list building in 2025–2026 (see the AI table). Extension at schema level is the outlier: MinistryPlatform alone lets a church define new tables ([MinistryPlatform](https://www.acstechnologies.com/ministryplatform/tools/platform/)).

### Follow-up, workflows and care: form-fed pipelines replaced process queues

Follow-up has become a pipeline feature. The leaders that reworked automation in 2025–2026 all moved toward boards of cards fed by forms: Workflows reached open beta at Subsplash in November 2025, a visual Workflows builder joined Pushpay's older process queues in September 2026, and form responses began triggering automations in Planning Center in June 2026 ([Subsplash](https://www.subsplash.com/blog/2025-subsplash-product-event-recap); [Pushpay](https://pushpay.com/product/releases/); [Planning Center](https://www.planningcenter.com/changelog)). Pastoral care is thinner: dedicated care apps, visit tracking and benevolence handling appear in only a few products. Consistent evaluation of spiritual growth is absent for **66% of the 2,620 US Protestant pastors** in one survey, which argues for simple steps and tasks over elaborate scoring ([Lifeway Research](https://news.lifeway.com/2025/10/07/most-churches-rarely-evaluate-their-discipleship-strategies/)).

### Groups: finders and leader tools are standard; confidentiality options are not

Group management is close to uniform: types, a public finder, sign-up modes and leader tools that work without full administrator rights. Planning Center stands alone in offering confidential groups, whose rosters only leaders and administrators see, and link-only groups ([Planning Center](https://www.planningcenter.com/groups); [Planning Center](https://www.planningcenter.com/changelog)). The need is real and under-served in practice: only **55% of surveyed US churches track who takes part in classes or small groups**, against 90% that track tithes ([Barna and Pushpay 2026 report](https://worldtrend.theblendingofthebody.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/barna-pushpay-state-of-church-tech-report-2026-pp.pdf)).

### Giving: recurring, wallets, text and statements are standard; tap and non-cash gifts are the new edge

Giving is the most uniform area and the commercial engine of the sector: Subsplash and Tithely price their giving products at $0 a month and earn on processing ([Subsplash](https://www.subsplash.com/pricing); [Tithely](https://get.tithe.ly/pricing)). ### Finance and accounting: a real ledger ships in a minority of products

A general ledger is the clearest dividing line in the market. It ships in Realm, the Ministry Brands line and a cluster of low-cost products, and is absent from Planning Center, Pushpay, Subsplash and TouchPoint, which export to accounting software instead ([Planning Center](https://www.planningcenter.com/giving); [Pushpay](https://pushpay.com/product/chms-software/); [TouchPoint](https://www.touchpointsoftware.com/church-finance-software/)). No open source church system includes one. Classes and sub-accounts stand in for designated funds wherever a church keeps its books in QuickBooks, a product not built for fund accounting ([Tithely](https://get.tithe.ly/blog/quickbooks-online-integration)); the valuable minimum for a product without a ledger is therefore a clean deposit or journal export with fund-to-account mapping and fee handling.

### Communications: email and SMS are standard; chat with safety rules is the fast-moving edge

Email and metered SMS are standard; what moved in 2025–2026 is chat. Planning Center, Realm, Subsplash and MinistryPlatform all run in-app group conversations; ChurchTrac and ChurchTools extended theirs during 2026, and Church Social added private replies and reactions ([Planning Center](https://www.planningcenter.com/chat); [ChurchTrac](https://www.churchtrac.com/blog/new-look-new-features); [ChurchTools](https://blog.church.tools/en/); [Church Social](https://churchsocial.com/changelog)). Chat brings child-safety design with it, and Planning Center's rules make the point: nobody under 13 gets in, a teenager is never in a conversation with fewer than two adults, and only adults can open a direct message ([Planning Center](https://www.planningcenter.com/chat)). Outside North America the channel mix differs, yet only ChMeetings and ChurchPlus touch WhatsApp ([ChMeetings](https://www.chmeetings.com/all-features/); [ChurchPlus](https://churchplus.co/)).

### Permissions, security and audit: roles are universal; enforced two-step verification is spreading

Role-based access is universal, and the security baseline tightened visibly in 2026. Since Realm's release of 11 November 2026, nobody holding responsibilities can sign in without a second step, and no new account goes live until an administrator approves it ([Realm](https://help.acst.com/en/realm/whats-new/2026/november-11-2026)). ## Compliance rules dictate data objects, not just policies

Law and accepted practice shape church software more than any competitor does. Each rule below turns into a record the system must hold, a state it must enforce or a file it must produce. The "feature it forces" columns are design inferences from the cited rules; the last column names products that already work this way.

### Tax receipting: every country ties the donor's relief to a different record

US law makes the church's paperwork the gatekeeper of the deduction: a single gift of **$250 or more** counts only when the donor holds a written acknowledgment, which may be one annual summary and may arrive by email ([IRS Publication 1771](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p1771.pdf)). A new deduction of up to $1,000 ($2,000 for joint filers) opens to non-itemisers from the 2026 tax year and covers cash gifts only, so statements that total cash and non-cash giving separately serve donors better ([Taft](https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/charitable-giving-after-the-obbba-the-2026-outlook/)). The UK works the other way round: the church reclaims the tax, so it must hold a valid declaration for each donor and trace every claimed gift to it, and the scheme leaves an estimated **£560 million** a year on the table, by HMRC's reckoning ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charities-detailed-guidance-notes/chapter-3-gift-aid); [HaysMac](https://haysmac.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Gift-Aid-2026.pdf)). Canada prescribes the receipt itself, serial number included, and insists that the records sit at the charity's Canadian address ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/books-records.html)). A receipting engine therefore needs rule sets and templates per country, not one generic statement.

### Payments: hosted fields, stored mandates and no card numbers in the admin screen

A processor-hosted form is what keeps card data away from church servers; only a token, the card brand, the last four digits and the expiry date need to be stored ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/security/guide)). Outsourcing no longer ends the obligation: two PCI DSS requirements on payment-page scripts took effect on **31 March 2025**, and the burden of showing that the surrounding page cannot be used for a script attack now falls on any merchant that embeds a provider's form ([PCI Security Standards Council](https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/coffee-with-the-council-podcast-guidance-for-pci-dss-e-commerce-requirements-effective-after-31-march-2025)). Rock RMS set the open source precedent: tokenised gateways are the only kind its core payment blocks accept, and card-swipe kiosks disappeared from the product once their compliance burden proved unsustainable ([Rock RMS](https://community.rockrms.com/connect/ending-support-for-legacy-financial-gateways)). Planning Center's guidance points the same way: donors, never staff, should type in card or account numbers, and the product gives administrators no way to add a bank account ([Planning Center](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/payment-methods.html)).

| Rule or practice | Feature it forces |
|---|---|
| Card details sent straight from the browser to the processor keep the church out of most PCI controls; brand, last four digits and expiry may be stored ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/security/guide)) | Gateway plug-ins that render processor-hosted fields or redirect; tokens only; no admin form that accepts a card or bank number |
| Pages hosting an embedded payment form must be shown to resist script attacks ([PCI Security Standards Council](https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/coffee-with-the-council-podcast-guidance-for-pci-dss-e-commerce-requirements-effective-after-31-march-2025)) | Strict Content Security Policy and no third-party scripts on giving pages; written guidance for self-hosters on the SAQ A confirmation |
| US internet-initiated bank debits need account validation, and every originator falls under fraud-monitoring duties by 19 June 2026 ([Nacha](https://nacha.org/rules/supplementing-fraud-detection-standards-web-debits); [Nacha](https://www.nacha.org/rules/risk-management-topics-fraud-monitoring-phase-1)) | Bank verification through the gateway; signed webhooks and a scheduled transaction download for reconciliation |

### Privacy: nearly every membership record is special-category data

A membership list counts as sensitive under European law, since belonging to a church discloses belief. The not-for-profit condition is the usual legal footing, and it has edges: it reaches members, former members and regular contacts, stops at employees and prospective members on the UK regulator's reading, and lapses as soon as data leaves the body without consent ([GDPR Article 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/); [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-are-the-conditions-for-processing/)). The pressure is growing. A case on baptism registers is before the EU's top court, and the Advocate General's non-binding Opinion of 1 October 2026 sided with a former member: unless the church shows compelling grounds, the entry has to stop identifying the person, and a line struck through legible text falls short; the judgment is still to come ([Court of Justice of the EU](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-10/cp260139en.pdf)). American churches face far less: nonprofits fall outside most state privacy statutes, Colorado's and New Jersey's being the notable ones without an exemption, so tooling built to the European standard covers the American cases as well ([PrivacyLawMap](https://privacylawmap.com/blog/state-privacy-laws-nonprofit-organizations-guide)).

### Child safety: matched codes, two adults, expiring credentials and a locked concern log

Insurers, accreditation bodies and safeguarding charities converge on the same controls. Children leave only with an authorised adult under a claim-check procedure, and two screened, unrelated adults stay in every room ([Brotherhood Mutual](https://brotherhoodmutual.com/safety-library/sexual-abuse-prevention-checklist); [Brotherhood Mutual](https://www.brotherhoodmutual.com/legalassist/legal-q-a/ministry-activities-and-operations/adult-to-minor-staffing-ratios/)). Re-screening intervals are where guidance splits: annual checks are one US insurer's rule, five years is a screening vendor's benchmark and three years is the Church of England's DBS cycle, so the renewal period has to be configurable ([Church Mutual](https://www.churchmutual.com/13472/Why-Background-Checks-Matter); [Protect My Ministry](https://www.protectmyministry.com/blog/when-should-your-church-re-screen-employees-and-volunteers/); [Diocese of Canterbury](https://canterburydiocese.org/safer-recruitment-and-dbs.php)). English law tightened in 2026. Supervision no longer exempts a volunteer under the Crime and Policing Act: regular teaching or supervising of children now counts as regulated activity and calls for an enhanced check, and a new duty to report child sexual abuse is on the statute book with its start date still awaiting guidance ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/crime-and-policing-act-2026-factsheets/crime-and-policing-act-2026-independent-inquiry-into-child-sexual-abuse-recommendations-factsheet)).

### Security: six incidents show which defaults matter

The dominant failures since 2020 were ordinary ones, and each maps to a default a self-hosted product can ship. Vendor risk is now part of the standard advice to churches: nearly half the breaches (48%) in the 2026 Verizon dataset ran through a third party, which is why Church Law & Tax supplies questions on enforced multi-factor sign-in, encryption and breach reporting to put to every supplier ([Church Law & Tax](https://churchleaders.com/ministry-insiders/church-law-tax/2225108-cybersecurity-risks.html/3)).

| Incident | What failed | Default it argues for |
|---|---|---|
| Blackbaud, 2020: data taken from more than 13,000 customer organisations ([The Record](https://therecord.media/blackbaud-must-pay-settlement-california)) | One compromised login, weak segmentation, backups kept without need | Two-factor sign-in enforced for privileged roles; backup pruning and a retention schedule |
| Cornerstone Payment Systems, 2022: 9,098,506 transaction records exposed ([Website Planet](https://www.websiteplanet.com/blog/cornerstone-leak-report/)) | Database with no password | Authentication on every datastore; minimal stored payment data |
| ChurchCRM CVE-2025-68110, rated 10.0 ([OffSeq](https://radar.offseq.com/threat/cve-2025-68110-cwe-200-exposure-of-sensitive-infor-dac7d6cf)) | Database credentials revealed in error messages | No connection details or stack traces in production errors |
| Click To Pray, 2026: records of about 700,000 users enumerable ([Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/privacy/2026/07/vaticans-click-to-pray-app-exposed-personal-data-from-700000-users)) | API returned any user's record; sequential identifiers; unanswered reports | Authorisation on every API object; non-sequential public identifiers; a monitored security contact |
| Beacon CRM, 2026: supporter data of more than 1,000 nonprofits ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/amp/cybersecurity-breach-exposes-donor-data/)) | Intruder copied database backups at an ISO 27001-certified vendor | Encrypted, access-restricted backups; certification is no substitute for controls |
| Seoul megachurches, 2026: a dataset on about 850,000 members ([The Record](https://therecord.media/south-korea-hackers-megachurches)) | Web shell, leaked passwords, password-reset flaw | Hardened upload and reset paths; rate limiting; no shared administrator credentials |

### Messaging, accessibility, licensing and financial controls round out the rule set

Four further rule families generate features. Messaging law everywhere demands recorded consent and a working opt-out; in the United States carrier registration is a precondition as well, since texts from a church without its own registered brand and campaign are liable to be filtered before delivery ([Twilio](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/4405850570267-Nonprofit-and-Government-Guide-to-A2P-10DLC-Text-Messaging); [Text In Church](https://help.textinchurch.com/en/articles/7670784-sender-profile-us-accounts-only)). Accessibility law mostly passes US churches by, since the ADA's public-accommodation title excludes religious organisations, but WCAG 2.2 is the working standard and European e-commerce has been subject to the Accessibility Act since 28 June 2025 ([Cornell Legal Information Institute](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/12187); [Siteimprove](https://siteimprove.com/en/blog/the-european-accessibility-act-frequently-asked-questions/)). Song licences come with usage reporting, and every finance guide consulted splits authorising, holding, recording and reconciling money among different people ([CCLI](https://support-ccli-us.helpscoutdocs.com/article/411-how-often-do-i-need-to-report); [Charity Commission](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/internal-financial-controls-for-charities-cc8/internal-financial-controls-for-charities)).

| Rule | Feature it forces |
|---|---|
| US email: opt-outs honoured within 10 business days and a postal address in commercial messages ([Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)) | One-click unsubscribe, global suppression list, sender identity and postal address in every bulk email |
| US texting: prior express consent, STOP-type replies revoke it, carrier registration of brand and campaign ([47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200); [Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/legal/messaging-policy)) | Per-channel consent with source and timestamp; automatic STOP handling; fields for the church's own registration; bring-your-own SMS provider |
| UK charities may market electronically on a soft opt-in since 5 February 2026; Canadian implied consent lasts two years; Australian unsubscribes take effect within 5 working days ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/the-charitable-purpose-soft-opt-in/); [CRTC](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm); [ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam)) | Consent basis, collection date and expiry stored per contact; proof of consent retained |
| WCAG 2.2 is the current W3C recommendation ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/)) | Public pages (giving, forms, registration, portal, kiosk) built to WCAG 2.2 AA |
| Software connected to CCLI reports song use continuously; streamed services need an add-on licence and attribution ([CCLI](https://support-ccli-us.helpscoutdocs.com/article/411-how-often-do-i-need-to-report); [CCLI](https://support-ccli-us.helpscoutdocs.com/article/1500-what-acknowledgement-should-i-use-for-my-streaming-activity)) | Song records with CCLI number, authors and copyright line; licence numbers; usage counts by type; report export; generated attribution text |

| Every transaction involves more than one person and leaves an audit trail ([Charity Commission](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/internal-financial-controls-for-charities-cc8/internal-financial-controls-for-charities)) | Immutable history of edits to gifts, donors, declarations and receipts; soft deletion; locks once statements or claims are issued |

## Open source covers slices, and no project covers the whole

Coverage approaching a commercial suite exists in just three actively developed projects, and each carries a structural catch. Rock RMS is the broadest, but only faith-based 501(c)(3) organisations may run it under the Rock Community License, hosting it as a managed service for others is off the table, and a Windows Server with IIS and SQL Server is a precondition ([Rock RMS](https://www.rockrms.com/license); [Rock RMS](https://community.rockrms.com/documentation/bookcontent/1)). ChurchApps B1 carries an MIT licence and counts **14,764+ churches**, nearly all on its free hosted service; the beta tag still hangs on its one-command Docker install ([ChurchApps](https://churchapps.org/); [ChurchApps](https://github.com/ChurchApps/B1Admin)). Churchit, begun in September 2025 on the Frappe framework, already has check-in with pickup codes, paid registration, volunteer scheduling, service plans and a member portal, but nearly every commit comes from a single maintainer, whose README is candid about rough edges ([Churchit](https://github.com/meichthys/churchit)). Project health comes down to how few people carry each code base. Rock and CiviCRM are the only projects whose 2025–2026 history is not the work of one to three people: one name sits on roughly 4,340 of ChurchCRM's commits for the period, EcclesiaCRM and Churchit each rest on a single developer, and OpenPetra's entire 2025 output has one author ([ChurchCRM](https://github.com/ChurchCRM/CRM); [EcclesiaCRM](https://github.com/phili67/ecclesiacrm); [OpenPetra](https://github.com/openpetra/openpetra)). Administrators, for their part, complain about technical burden more than about missing features. Rock scores 4.9 out of 5 across 105 Capterra reviews, and those same reviewers cite the learning curve, hard-to-navigate documentation and the Windows requirement ([Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/141654/Rock-RMS/reviews/)). ChurchCRM published **ten security advisories between 10 and 14 August 2026**, among them a critical bypass of its two-factor lockout and a SQL injection, and two of its last four releases carried upgrade warnings ([ChurchCRM](https://github.com/ChurchCRM/CRM/security/advisories); [ChurchCRM](https://github.com/ChurchCRM/CRM/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)).

Free software already does three neighbouring jobs well enough that LibreEkklesia should connect to it instead of rebuilding it: projection (OpenLP, Quelea, FreeShow), donation and booking building blocks (GiveWP, LibreBooking) and hierarchical membership management (hitobito) ([OpenLP](https://openlp.org/); [Quelea](https://quelea.org/); [FreeShow](https://freeshow.app/); [GiveWP](https://github.com/impress-org/givewp); [LibreBooking](https://github.com/LibreBooking/app); [hitobito](https://github.com/hitobito/hitobito)).

The project name deserves a check before launch, because church software already holds several near neighbours: Ekklesia 360, an established website builder in the Ministry Brands family with an announced e360 ChMS; EcclesiaCRM, an active open source church CRM; and LibreChurch, a German free-software initiative for churches ([The Lead Pastor](https://theleadpastor.com/tools/ekklesia360-review/); [Ekklesia 360](https://hello.ekklesia360.com/blog/announcing-e360-chms); [EcclesiaCRM](https://github.com/phili67/ecclesiacrm); [LibreChurch](https://librechurch.org/)). The exact name LibreEkklesia appears to be unused, although trademark, domain and package registries were not searched.

## Buyers want simple, connected and portable; vendors spent 2025–2026 shipping AI

### Ease of use, price and trust outrank feature depth

Churches do not choose software on feature count. Asked what decides a purchase, the 1,306 US church leaders in Barna's 2026 survey for Pushpay put ease of use at the top, then price (extremely important to 66%), reputation (61%) and data security (59%), leaving feature depth and integrations out of the first four ([Pushpay](https://pushpay.com/?p=19291); [Barna](https://production.barna.com/articles/church-technology-mission)). Two caveats apply: Pushpay has a commercial stake in the findings, and congregations under 200 supplied over half the responses ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/the-new-church-tech-divide-is-missional-not-digital/)). The typical operator is not a professional administrator: bivocational ministry is the reality for **35% of US clergy**, and a vendor compilation puts the median congregation near 60 regular participants ([The Baptist Paper](https://thebaptistpaper.org/study-evangelical-pastors-more-likely-to-be-bivocational/); [Subsplash](https://www.subsplash.com/blog/100-must-know-facts-about-technology-church-trends)).

Adoption figures show money tracked far better than people. Online giving is in use at 90% of surveyed churches, church management software at 84% and texting at 80%, yet only 62% track member and attender lists and 55% track group participants, against 90% for tithes ([Barna and Pushpay 2026 report](https://worldtrend.theblendingofthebody.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/barna-pushpay-state-of-church-tech-report-2026-pp.pdf)). Confidence that church data could yield meaningful AI insight is rare, at **19% of leaders** ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/09/3251986/0/en/Pushpay-and-Barna-Group-s-2026-State-of-Church-Technology-Report-Shows-Church-Tech-Has-Moved-Past-Adoption-Now-Alignment-Is-What-Matters.html)). The larger unmet need is therefore on the people side, where capturing lists and participation still takes effort.

### Five complaints recur: fragmentation, complexity, reporting, price opacity and lock-in

The same grievances surface in advisor calls, review sites and forums. Buyers who spoke to Software Advice's advisors in 95 conversations between January 2025 and July 2026 mostly arrived with four problems: scattered systems and spreadsheets, clumsy interfaces, thin reporting and prices that keep climbing; a QuickBooks Online link was among their most frequent requests ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/church/)). Messaging failures and weak mobile access join that list on G2 ([G2](https://www.g2.com/categories/church-management)). Complexity has a visible cost: a full-time database administrator was the price of getting value from ChurchStaq for one reviewer's church, and Capterra's averages run from 3.4 out of 5 for FellowshipOne to 4.8 for Tithely Church Management ([Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/211982/ChuchStaq/); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/230779/Fellowship-One/reviews/?page=2); [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/132513/Breeze-ChMS/reviews/)). Label printers and Sunday-morning connectivity, not missing functions, draw most of the check-in criticism ([Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/146265/Realm/reviews/); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/76708/Planning-Center/reviews/?page=5)).

Price and lock-in are trust problems as much as budget problems. Pushpay sells only through quotes on contracts of one to three years with the balance due on early exit ([Pushpay](https://pushpay.com/product/pricing/)). The sharpest lock-in lesson is Blackbaud's exit from the category: its church product went dark on 22 January 2024, and with it went every customer's login and access to their data ([Blackbaud](https://blackbaud.com/blackbaud-church-management-update/)). What churches ask for in return is modest. One affordable, self-hostable progressive web app for events, groups, volunteering, streaming and giving was the wish of a 2023 forum thread, and the replies supplied the catch: hosting skills are exactly what small churches do not have ([Tildes](https://tildes.net/~tech/171v/free_and_or_open_source_software_alternatives_for_churches)). One long-time Planning Center user's published wish list runs to quick capture by voice or text, care boards, cross-module tasks and a built-in wiki ([Thomas Harmond](https://thomasharmond.substack.com/p/on-planning-center-home)).

Price anchors are low. Roughly $0 to $35 a month buys a complete commercial system for a congregation of under 100: the free ChMeetings tier stops at 50 people and its next step costs $12 a month for 100, ChurchTrac's $29 plan includes accounting, and £32.50 covers ChurchSuite's full suite for fewer than 100 contacts ([ChMeetings](https://www.chmeetings.com/?p=32233); [ChurchTrac](https://www.churchtrac.com/pricing); [ChurchSuite](https://churchsuite.com/pricing/)). Tithely Church Management charges a flat **$72 a month** for unlimited people, and advisor-call data put the median quoted price at $100 a month ([Tithely](https://get.tithe.ly/church-management?r=tlp-ocms); [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/church/)). The cost incumbents cite against open source is hosting: a competitor estimates Rock RMS at roughly $50 to $200 a month plus developer time ([Pushpay](https://pushpay.com/blog/the-costs-of-free-church-management-software/)).

Needs outside the United States differ in rails and channels more than in categories. European buyers weigh hosting location and national tax schemes because membership data is sensitive, though the directory making that case accepts paid placement ([European Purpose](https://europeanpurpose.com/category/church-management-software)). Mobile-money transactions passed $2 trillion in 2025, and a Kenyan guide describes the usual set-up as a church-owned M-Pesa paybill with the member number as the account reference, which makes statement import and reconciliation by reference code a simpler first step than live payment integration ([GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/mobile-money-accounted-for-2-trillion-in-transactions-in-2025-doubling-since-2021-as-active-accounts-continue-to-grow/); [PaybillKE](https://paybillke.com/guides/mpesa-for-churches-and-fundraising-2026)). Beyond that, what is known about church needs in Africa, Latin America and Asia comes from vendors without sources; independent survey data is missing.

Supply is running ahead of demand on AI. Church-level use is reported by only a third of surveyed leaders (33%); what use there is clusters in graphics (36%) and written content (31%), with reporting at 7% ([Barna and Pushpay 2026 report](https://worldtrend.theblendingofthebody.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/barna-pushpay-state-of-church-tech-report-2026-pp.pdf)). Data privacy in AI worries 83% of them at least somewhat, and the gap between the 64% who consider an AI policy important and the 5% who have one is wide. Regular AI use stands at 10% among US Protestant pastors in a separate Lifeway study ([Religion Unplugged](https://religionunplugged.com/news/2026/4/21/pastors-churchgoers-see-ai-as-concerning-and-confusing)). Vendor guardrails (permission scoping, no training on church data, opt-in access) read as direct answers to those worries, and they help explain why the connector model, in which the church brings its own assistant, became the 2026 pattern in place of proprietary chatbots.

Market-size figures deserve scepticism. Published estimates for 2025 range from $292 million to $851 million to $17.23 billion ([SkyQuest](https://www.giiresearch.com/report/sky1914104-church-management-software-market-size-share.html); [Technavio](https://www.technavio.com/report/church-management-software-market-industry-analysis); [Spherical Insights](https://www.sphericalinsights.com/reports/church-management-software-market)). An $800 million price for Subsplash, one of the largest vendors with more than 20,000 customers, makes the estimates in the hundreds of millions credible and the largest one implausible ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/church-tech-company-subsplash-to-be-acquired-by-roper-technologies)).

## Conclusion

The catalog changes the question LibreEkklesia has to answer. Parity is a known quantity, the standard rows, and none of it is technically exotic; what no open source project has done is deliver that floor under an OSI licence, on Linux and Docker, with giving and check-in in the core and upgrades that do not break. The edges worth choosing are those where openness is a structural advantage and incumbents are structurally weak: complete import and export, including the groups, attendance, notes and household roles that commercial migrations drop; church-owned payment and messaging providers behind a plug-in layer; a per-country receipting engine; consent, erasure and safeguarding records as first-class modules; public pages built to WCAG 2.2 AA in more than one language; check-in that tolerates a slow Sunday connection; and an API with an MCP server in place of a hosted model. The areas to leave to others are just as clear: projection, video hosting, payroll and, at least at first, a full ledger.

Two risks decide whether that position holds. Self-hosting skill is scarcest in exactly the congregations that want free software most, so a one-command install or a low-cost hosted option belongs in the product definition, not on the roadmap. And several inputs are still moving: the EU court's ruling on baptism registers, the start date of England's reporting duty and Pushpay's announced spring 2027 releases will each shift what counts as standard ([Pushpay](https://hub.pushpay.com/staq-transformation/)). A catalog built from vendor pages also cannot say which features churches use, since no survey measures that, so the project's own early adopters will supply the first real evidence on the question.
